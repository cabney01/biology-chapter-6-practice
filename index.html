1. Learn the big picture

DNA stores instructions. The cell makes an RNA copy of the needed instructions, and ribosomes use the RNA message to assemble amino acids into a protein.

Important distinction: DNA replication copies DNA for cell division. Transcription makes RNA from DNA. Translation uses mRNA to build a protein.

2. DNA and RNA: know the differences

DNAUsually double-stranded
Sugar: deoxyribose
Bases: A, T, C, G
A pairs with T; C pairs with G
RNAUsually single-stranded
Sugar: ribose
Bases: A, U, C, G
U replaces T

3. How the ribosome builds a protein

The ribosome reads mRNA in groups of three bases called codons. A tRNA anticodon pairs with the mRNA codon. The ribosome links the delivered amino acids with peptide bonds. AUG is the common start codon (methionine). UAA, UAG, and UGA are stop codons.

4. More Chapter 6 review

ATP and ADPATP stores usable energy temporarily. ATP + water → ADP + phosphate releases energy for cellular work. Cells recycle ADP to make more ATP.
MetabolismAll the chemical processes used to obtain, store, and use energy. Anabolism builds molecules; catabolism breaks molecules down.
Replication and mutationsDNA polymerase helps copy DNA. Errors in DNA replication may lead to mutations.
RNA processingIn eukaryotic cells, introns are removed and exons are joined before mature mRNA leaves the nucleus through nuclear pores.

Memory helper: mRNA = message; tRNA = transfer amino acids; rRNA = ribosome component. Autotrophs make their own food.
